# Preregistration addendum 5: grammar-constrained decoding against the repair loop (2026-09-09)

Written before any E4 call. Addenda 1 and 4 left E4 open because the hosted models expose no grammar path. This addendum runs it on a local CPU build of llama.cpp, which does. The question is the one every technical reader of the book has asked: on rules a grammar can express, what does a repair loop buy over enforcing the rule during generation?

## E4. Grammar-constrained decoding, the repair loop and verifier-selected resampling on one local base

Serving path: llama.cpp release b10883, the CPU-only Windows build, run as llama-server on the author's workstation at reduced priority, on a port and process of its own. No GPU is used. The base is Llama 3.1 8B Instruct in the Q4_K_M GGUF quantization published by bartowski; its SHA-256 is recorded in FREEZE-5.txt. This is the same model family and size as E3's small base but a quantized local copy, not the hosted bf16 model, so E4's arms are compared with each other and not with E3's numbers. Temperature 0.7 and a 400-token ceiling as in the earlier addenda; the server's other sampling settings stay at their defaults and are recorded from the first response into results-e4.json. Prompts go through the model's own chat template (the server's apply-template endpoint) and the completion endpoint, which accepts a GBNF grammar per request.

Task set: the kit's 39 self-authored constraints (src/organs/instruction-following/constraints.ts at commit 3d20393), with their checkers as the judge for every arm, exactly as in E1 to E3.

Grammars: one GBNF grammar per constraint, generated by make-grammars.py and frozen in grammars.json (hash in FREEZE-5.txt). Each grammar encodes one reading of its checker: a word is a run of non-whitespace characters separated by single spaces; a sentence is a run of characters without terminal punctuation followed by one of . ! ?; forbidden letters are excluded from the character classes in both cases; JSON shapes are spelled out key by key; bullet lists are lines with the required marker; the three word-frequency tasks use a product construction that counts the target word (case-insensitive, whole word) and the sentences at once, with words restricted to letters. Every grammar was tested offline against 40 strings that must be accepted and 60 that must be rejected, using a small GBNF matcher, with no model call. Where a grammar is stricter than the checker (no commas or apostrophes in the word-frequency tasks, no abbreviations inside sentences), that is a cost of the approach and is not corrected after the fact. Where a grammar's reading differs from the checker's, the checker wins and the constrained arm fails the item.

Arms per item and repetition, first draft shared between the repair and resampling arms as in E2 and E3:

1. One-shot: the prompt plus the kit's first-shot suffix, one generation, no grammar.
2. Repair: the kit's runConstraintRepair at commit 3d20393, maxRetries 3 (a ceiling of four generations), continuing from the shared first draft, re-prompting with the specific violation, stopping at a pass.
3. Verifier-selected resampling: three further unguided generations of the same first-shot prompt; the item passes if any of the four passes the checker.
4. Constrained: the same first-shot prompt, one generation, with the item's grammar enforced during decoding.

Three repetitions of the whole design over the 39 items, arms in the order above, every call logged raw with prompt, answer, token counts, elapsed milliseconds, seed, stop reason and the error field. Seeds are derived deterministically from repetition, item, arm and draw.

Primary outcome: constrained minus repair, pass rate under the kit checker over the 39 items, pooled over the three repetitions by a cluster bootstrap that resamples items and carries their repetitions (2,000 resamples, seed 12345).

Kill rule: if the interval includes zero, the book states that on the self-authored set grammar-constrained decoding and the repair loop did not separate. If the interval lies above zero, the book states that constrained decoding beat the repair loop at one generation against a ceiling of four, on the rules a grammar could express. If it lies below zero, the repair loop beat constrained decoding, and the book says so.

Secondary, all reported as descriptive intervals without adjustment: constrained minus resampling; constrained minus one-shot; repair minus one-shot and repair minus resampling on this local quantized base (a replication of E2's design on a different base, reported beside E3's hosted 8B without pooling); pass rates by constraint class (word count, forbidden letter, sentence count, JSON shape, bullet lines, casing, sentence-initial letter, comma count, word frequency, ending); generations and generated tokens per arm; wall-clock seconds per arm on this hardware; the number of constrained generations that hit the 400-token ceiling or returned a server error; and a topic-keyword proxy per arm, the fraction of answers containing the topic word named in the prompt, computed by the frozen rule in e4.mjs and the map in keywords.json. The rule finds no topic phrase for six tasks (the two JSON shapes, the two bullet lists, the mountain description and the photosynthesis explanation), which are excluded from the proxy. The proxy is a crude check that constrained answers stayed on the subject; it is not a quality measure and the book will not call it one.

Predictions written now: constrained decoding near 100 percent on word count, forbidden letter, casing, bullet, JSON, comma and sentence-count classes; below that on word frequency, where the product grammar may drive the model into repetitive or off-topic text that still passes; repair above one-shot and below constrained overall; repair ahead of resampling on exact counts and behind it on forbidden letters, as in E2; a visible share of constrained answers that pass the checker while reading worse than the unconstrained ones, which the topic proxy will catch only partly.

Limits stated in advance: one local quantized base on CPU; the grammars are the authors' reading of their own checkers; the constraint set is self-authored; wall-clock cost is hardware-specific and is not a price. E4 measures format compliance under a machine checker and nothing about the quality of the prose.

Smoke test: one bare call and one grammar-constrained call on the first task before the run, to confirm the server, the template endpoint and grammar parsing. They are logged to raw/smoke.jsonl and excluded from every count.

## Reporting

E4 is reported in the evidence appendix beside the other controls under the frozen verdict, with the grammar coverage (39 of 39 constraints expressible, with the strictness costs named), the per-class table, the topic proxy, and the raw-log location. The repair chapter's sentence naming E4 as unrun is replaced by the result. If the frozen rule gives constrained decoding the win, the book says that the repair loop earns its place only where no grammar can be written, and Paper 1 is scoped accordingly.
